Lesson 5 · 15 min

Away from your computer: approve on your phone and send work to the cloud

Big projects don’t have to keep you in your chair. With Remote Control, you can follow a session running on your computer from your phone. With Projects, research runs in the cloud, even with your laptop closed. In this lesson, Carla approves a plan from the sofa and sends research to the cloud, knowing what leaves her computer.

Start the lesson

In this lesson, you’ll

  1. 1Three places where work can run
  2. 2Turn on Remote Control and approve from your phone
  3. 3Send research to Projects in the cloud
  4. 4Decide what can leave your computer

Goal: Follow and approve work from your phone, and send research to the cloud, knowing what leaves your computer.

  1. 1/5

    On your computer

    This is the way you already know: the session runs on your computer, and you follow along on the screen. Session = a conversation with Claude and its own history. If you get up from your chair, the work continues, but you won’t see when it stops and asks for your approval.

  2. 2/5

    Remote Control

    The session stays on your computer, but you can follow along and approve from the Claude app on your phone. You don’t need a terminal on your phone. One rule stays the same: if the computer goes to sleep or shuts down, the work stops.

  3. 3/5

    Projects

    The new Projects feature in Claude Code is an ongoing conversation where Claude opens a thread (a workstream) for each task. Threads run in the cloud, so you can close your laptop. The tradeoff is that the files you deliver leave your computer. It’s in beta, with a waitlist, on the Pro, Max, and Ultra plans.

  4. 4/5

    Claude Duet

    Duet is an assistant that brings you pending work from your projects, prepares a draft, and asks before taking action. For now, it’s only in the U.S., in preview, with a waitlist (Pro, Max, and Ultra). It hasn’t arrived in Brazil yet; just know that it exists.

  5. 5/5

    You choose where it runs

    Task with a confidential file: on your computer, using Remote Control to approve from afar. Research with public data: in the cloud, with your computer off.

From your computer to your phone, from your phone to the cloud: each step frees you from your chair and calls for a new decision about your files.

02 · Turn on Remote Control

It works in the computer app (Code tab) and in the terminal. You only need the Claude app on your phone.

1. Ask for a plan

Open a session in the work folder (you choose the folder when opening the session, as in lesson 2) and include a pause point in your request: "show me the plan and wait for my OK".

That way, Claude works up to the decision point and waits for you instead of continuing on its own.

CarlaAsks for the lesson 2 script for her spreadsheet course, with a plan first.

2. Turn on control

In the session message box (where you write requests), type /remote-control (or the shortcut /rc) and send it. In the computer app, you can also turn it on from the session’s Environment card.

Done: the session appears in the Claude app on your phone.

No terminalIn the app’s Code tab, type /remote-control in the message box, or use the session’s Environment card.

3. Keep

The work runs on your computer. If it goes to sleep, everything stops.

In the app, go to Settings → Code and turn on "Keep this computer awake." Keep your laptop plugged in.

Common mistakeClosing the laptop lid, thinking your phone will keep the work going.

4. Approve from your phone

Open the session in the Claude app on your phone, read the plan, and reply: approve it, ask for an adjustment, or tell it to stop.

Then let it work. Checking your phone every five minutes won’t make the work go faster.

TakeawayYou decide from afar; the computer does the work.

04 · Projects: work that continues with your laptop closed

Beta with a waitlist for Pro, Max, and Ultra plans. If it doesn’t show up for you, read and save the prompt.

1. Find Projects

Look for Projects in the sidebar of claude.ai/code or in the app’s Code tab. Don’t see it? Join the waitlist.

Heads up: this isn’t the old "Projects" feature in claude.ai chat, which is a folder for conversations with files. They’re different things.

2. Decide what to upload

Threads run in the cloud. Anything you give the project leaves your computer.

Simple rule: research using public information can be uploaded. A spreadsheet with students’ names and grades, a contract, or customer data stays in a local folder.

CarlaUpload: research on spreadsheet updates. Keep on your computer: the list of students and grades.

3. Ask and keep track

Describe the task in your own words. Claude opens a thread (a workstream) for each part and shows them in Overview (the overview), the panel alongside it. A dot appears when a thread is waiting for you.

Coordinating many threads is covered in the course Claude as an agent manager.

4. Get the file

The result is in the project’s Library tab. Open the file, check the source links, and only then use it.

Cloud research can be wrong too: broken links and incorrect dates happen. It’s up to you to check.

06 · Practice: approve from a distance

Do E1 or E2 (just one, depending on what you have) and E3, which everyone can do.

E1 · Approve a plan from your phone

20 min

Open a session in the folder for one of your projects, ask for a task with "show me the plan and wait for my OK," and turn on control: type /remote-control in the session’s message box (or use the Environment card in the app). Also turn on "Keep this computer awake" in Settings → Code. Step away from the computer and reply from your phone.

Done when: your approval (or adjustment), sent from your phone, appears in the session history, and the work continued afterward.

E2 · Cloud research

15 min

If Projects is available to you, create a project and request research using public information, based on Carla’s prompt. Close your laptop and come back later.

Done when: there’s a pesquisa.md file in the project’s Library, and you opened 2 source links to check that they work.

E3 · The list of things that stay off the cloud

5 min

Write down three types of files from your work that will never go to the cloud and where they stay on your computer.

Done when: You have a list with 3 file types and each one’s local folder.
